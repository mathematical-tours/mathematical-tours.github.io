\chapter*{About this book}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About this book}
This book offers five introductions to mathematical ideas that help us represent, process, and learn from data. Concrete examples provide a starting point, while precise statements explain what an algorithm can and cannot guarantee.
\begin{itemize}
\item Chapter~\ref{chap-shannon} introduces binary coding and Shannon's entropy bound for lossless compression.
\item Chapter~\ref{chap-images} treats an image as an array of numbers and develops quantization, filtering, edge detection, and color transformations.
\item Chapter~\ref{chap-sparsity} connects sampling and sparse approximation to the regularization of inverse problems and compressed sensing.
\item Chapter~\ref{chap-ot} develops optimal transport, from finite assignments to the comparison and interpolation of distributions.
\item Chapter~\ref{chap-neural-networks} explains discriminative and generative neural networks, their training objectives, and their connection with transport.
\end{itemize}
The first two chapters require elementary algebra and, for information theory, logarithms and basic probabilities. The later chapters use vectors, matrices, derivatives, and probability distributions. Their more advanced results are stated with their assumptions; the surrounding examples can be read before studying those results in detail.

Each chapter can be read separately. Reading them in order reveals useful connections: entropy explains coding, transforms make signals compressible, regularization selects reconstructions, and transport compares the distributions that a generative model seeks to learn. A common notation and short chapter introductions support both reading paths.

The English and French editions contain the same five chapters. Complete books, individual chapters, and the sources are available at
\begin{center}\url{https://mathematical-tours.github.io/book-basics/}\end{center}
For a more extensive treatment, see \emph{Mathematical Foundations of Data Sciences}, available from \url{https://mathematical-tours.github.io/book/}.
